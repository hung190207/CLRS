# Exercise 2.3-3

Use mathematical induction to show that when *n* is an exact power of 2, the solution of the recurrence

$$
T(n) =
\begin{cases}
2, & \text{if } n = 2, \\
2T(n/2) + n, & \text{if } n = 2^k,  k > 1.
\end{cases}
$$

is $T(n) = nlgn$.

# Answer

We prove that for every $n = 2^k$ with $k \ge 1$,

$$
T(n) = nlgn.
$$

### 1. Base Case

When $n = 2$:

$$
T(2) = 2
$$

Right-hand side:

$$
2 \lg 2 = 2 * 1 = 2
$$

=> Base case matches.

### 2. Induction Hypothesis

Assume the formula holds for some $n = 2^k$:

$$
T(n) = n \lg n.
$$

We must show it holds for $2n = 2^{k+1}$.

### 3. Induction Step

We use the recurrence for $T(2n)$:

$$
T(2n) = 2T(n) + 2n
$$

Substitute the induction hypothesis $T(n) = n \lg n$:

$$
T(2n) = 2(n \lg n)  + 2n
$$

Factor 2n:

$$
T(2n) = 2n(\lg n + 1)
$$

We have: $\lg(2n) = \lg 2 + lg n = 1 + lg n$

So:

$$
T(2n) = 2n \lg(2n)
$$

Since $2n = n'$, this becomes:

$$
T(n') = n' \lg n'
$$

The formula holds for $2n$.

## Conclusion

By mathematical induction, the solution to the recurrence

$$
T(n) = 2T(n/2) + n
$$

for exact powers of 2 is:

$$\boxed{
T(n) = n \lg n.
}$$
